# Lexerd Fit-Scoring Rubric — 3M Model

**Purpose:** Transparent, quantitative scoring framework for evaluating multifamily opportunities against Lexerd's investment thesis (Market/Model/Management).

**Output:** 0–100 fit score + confidence grade (A/B/C/D) + detailed breakdown for decision-making.

**Usage:** Apply this rubric to every deal prospect to assess thesis alignment before moving to diligence.

---

## Quick Reference

| Fit Score | Grade | Interpretation | Action |
|-----------|-------|---|---|
| 90–100 | **A** | Exceptional fit. Minimal risk, clear execution path. | 🟢 GREEN — Fast-track to diligence |
| 75–89 | **B** | Good fit. Meets thesis criteria; some areas need attention. | 🟡 YELLOW — Full diligence; manageable risks |
| 60–74 | **C** | Moderate fit. Requires deeper due diligence; thesis misalignment in 1–2 areas. | 🟠 ORANGE — Vet carefully; may pass |
| <60 | **D** | Weak fit. Does not align with Lexerd's thesis; significant misalignment. | 🔴 RED — Pass unless major thesis shift |

---

## Scoring Formula

```
Final Fit Score = (Market Score × 0.30) + (Model Score × 0.40) + (Management Score × 0.30)
```

**Interpretation:**
- 0.30 weight on Market: fundamentals matter, but are not destiny
- 0.40 weight on Model: Lexerd's operational edge is the primary value driver
- 0.30 weight on Management: execution probability is critical to realizing upside

## MARKET SCORE (30% Weight)

**Max 100 points.** Evaluate employment fundamentals, population trends, valuation, and anchor strength.

### 1. Employment Growth YoY (25 points max)

**Requirement:** Minimum 2.0% YoY employment growth (national avg: 1.0–1.5%)

**Scoring Logic:**
- 0.0–1.9% growth: **0 points** (below threshold)
- 2.0% growth: **10 points**
- 3.0% growth: **17 points**
- 4.0% growth: **25 points** (maximum)
- >4.0% growth: **25 points** (cap at 25)

**Why this matters:** Employment growth is the most reliable predictor of rent growth and occupancy stability. Properties in 3%+ markets historically outperform during value-add execution.

**Examples:**
- Fargo, ND (2.8% YoY): 14 pts
- Austin, TX (3.5% YoY): 25 pts
- Phoenix, AZ (2.1% YoY): 11 pts

### 2. Population Growth YoY (15 points max)

**Requirement:** Minimum 1.5% YoY population growth

**Scoring Logic:**
- 0.0–1.4% growth: **0 points**
- 1.5% growth: **6 points**
- 2.0% growth: **10 points**
- 2.5%+ growth: **15 points** (maximum)

**Why this matters:** Population growth signals in-migration and long-term demand. Markets losing population have structural headwinds (Rust Belt cities). Markets gaining population have tailwinds.

**Examples:**
- Boise, ID (2.3% YoY): 12 pts
- Bradenton, FL (2.1% YoY): 9 pts
- Jacksonville, FL (1.8% YoY): 7 pts

### 3. Cap-Rate Spread vs. National Average (30 points max)

**Requirement:** Minimum 200 bps above national average cap rate

**Scoring Logic:**
- <100 bps spread: **0 points** (expensive market)
- 100–199 bps spread: **10 points**
- 200–299 bps spread: **20 points**
- 300+ bps spread: **30 points** (maximum)

**Assumption:** National average cap rate = 6.0% (real data sourced from CoStar market reports).

**Formula:** Market cap rate = 6.0% + spread (bps) / 10,000

**Examples:**
- Secondary market trading at 7.5% cap rate = 150 bps spread = 10 pts
- Secondary market trading at 8.0% cap rate = 200 bps spread = 20 pts
- Distressed market trading at 9.0% cap rate = 300 bps spread = 30 pts

**Why this matters:** Cap-rate spread is the clearest valuation signal. Wider spread = lower purchase price for same NOI = more upside from value-add.

### 4. Employment Anchor Strength (30 points max)

**Requirement:** At least 1 anchor in top 5 employers

**Anchor Types:**
- Military bases (Fort Eisenhower, Fort Stewart, Quantico, etc.)
- Medical centers (regional hospitals, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic)
- Universities (large state schools, research institutions)
- Manufacturing hubs (automotive, aerospace, industrial)
- Tech clusters (emerging, not commoditized)

**Scoring Logic:**
- 0 anchors in top 5: **0 points**
- 1 anchor identified: **15 points**
- 2+ anchors identified: **30 points** (maximum)

**Examples:**
- Fargo: Military base (1) + Healthcare system (1) = 30 pts
- Jacksonville: Naval Station + Regional hospitals = 30 pts
- Raleigh: Universities (NCSU, Duke) + Tech = 30 pts
- Generic suburb with no anchor: 0 pts

**Why this matters:** Anchors provide structural, long-term demand. Properties in anchor markets weather downturns better.

## MODEL SCORE (40% Weight)

**Max 100 points.** Evaluate property characteristics and value-add signals.

### 1. Unit Count (20 points max)

**Requirement:** 70–300 units (sweet spot: 150–250)

**Scoring Logic:**
- 70–300 units: **20 points** (in range)
- 50–69 units: **10 points** (slightly below)
- 301–400 units: **10 points** (slightly above)
- <50 or >400 units: **0 points** (out of range)

**Why:** 70–300 units is institutional PM-efficient scale. Too small = high per-unit costs; too large = complexity compounds.

### 2. Property Class (20 points max)

**Requirement:** Class B or B- (institutional, fixable)

**Scoring Logic:**
- B / B+: **20 points** (preferred)
- B- / B': **20 points** (acceptable, higher execution risk)
- A / A-: **0 points** (already optimized, low upside)
- C / C+: **0 points** (high capex, execution risk)

**Why:** Class B/B- is Lexerd's sweet spot — modern enough to not need major capex, old enough to have operational inefficiency. Class A is fully optimized. Class C requires major renovation.

### 3. Occupancy (20 points max)

**Requirement:** 80–95% (below-market is value-add signal)

**Scoring Logic:**
- 80–95% occupancy: **20 points** (sweet spot, upside signal)
- 75–79%: **10 points** (below target, higher risk)
- 96–99%: **10 points** (already optimized, limited upside)
- <75% or 100%: **0 points** (distressed or unrealistic)

**Market context:** Market average occupancy is typically 92–95%. Property at 85% occupancy has clear upside (bring to market = 92%).

**Examples:**
- 85% occupied in 93% market: 20 pts
- 97% occupied in 93% market: 5 pts (little upside)
- 70% occupied: 0 pts (too distressed)

### 4. Expense Ratio Gap (20 points max)

**Requirement:** Property expense ratio >5% above market benchmark

**Scoring Logic:**
- >5% above benchmark: **20 points** (clear upside)
- 3–5% above: **10 points** (moderate upside)
- 0–2% above: **5 points** (minimal upside)
- At or below benchmark: **0 points** (already optimized)

**Expense benchmarks** (by class and market):
- Class B secondary market benchmark: 27–28% of GRI
- Class B- secondary market benchmark: 28–30% of GRI

**Examples:**
- Property at 32% vs. 27% benchmark = 5% gap = 20 pts
- Property at 29% vs. 27% benchmark = 2% gap = 5 pts
- Property at 27% vs. 27% benchmark = 0% gap = 0 pts

**Why:** Expense ratio is the clearest operational signal. 5% gap = $50K+ annual NOI improvement on $1M rent base.

### 5. Rent Upside (20 points max)

**Requirement:** In-place rent below market (upside potential)

**Scoring Logic:**
- In-place rent <market rent: **20 points** (upside exists)
- In-place rent 0–2% above market: **10 points** (limited upside)
- In-place rent >2% above market: **0 points** (already optimized)

**Calculation:**
- Market average rent: $1,300/unit/month
- Property in-place rent: $1,200/unit/month
- Gap: ($1,300 - $1,200) / $1,300 = 7.7% below market
- **Score: 20 points** (clear upside)

**Why:** Below-market rent signals incumbent operator failure (weak leasing, poor tenant experience). Lexerd can correct this through PM replacement and pricing discipline.

## MANAGEMENT SCORE (30% Weight)

**Max 100 points.** Evaluate operator quality, integration fit, and execution probability.

### 1. Property Management Type (20 points max)

**Requirement:** Third-party managed (easier to replace)

**Scoring Logic:**
- Third-party managed: **20 points** (preferred, replaceable)
- Owner-managed but experienced: **10 points** (may resist change)
- Owner-managed, inexperienced: **5 points** (high resistance, execution risk)
- Complex arrangement (split management, family-owned): **0 points** (execution blocker)

**Why:** Third-party PM is a contractor and can be swapped. Owner-managed properties have cultural resistance to change.

### 2. First Communities Integration (20 points max)

**Requirement:** Property profile fits First Communities model

**Integration Fit Criteria:**
- Property type: Multifamily residential (yes) vs. mixed-use (harder)
- Size: 70–300 units (yes) vs. >500 units (different playbook)
- Complexity: Standard lease structure (yes) vs. specialized (co-op, resident-owned, etc.)
- Location: Mid-market secondary (yes) vs. high-barrier markets

**Scoring Logic:**
- Excellent fit (standard property, good size, clean structure): **20 points**
- Good fit (minor complications): **15 points**
- Moderate fit (one significant complication): **10 points**
- Poor fit (multiple complications): **0 points**

**Examples:**
- 180-unit Class B, standard leasing, Jacksonville: 20 pts (perfect fit)
- 220-unit Class B-, union labor, Atlanta: 10 pts (complicates but doable)
- 400-unit Class A, luxury amenities, Miami: 0 pts (not First Communities model)

### 3. Lory Rebranding Playbook (20 points max)

**Requirement:** Property profile matches Lory historical successes

**Lory Playbook Fit:**
- Class: B / B- (yes) vs. A or C (no)
- Units: 70–300 (yes) vs. outside range (no)
- PM model: Replaceable (yes) vs. complex (no)
- Market: Secondary, growth-oriented (yes) vs. flat/declining (no)

**Scoring Logic:**
- Excellent fit (all criteria met): **20 points**
- Good fit (4/5 criteria met): **15 points**
- Moderate fit (3/5 criteria met): **10 points**
- Poor fit (≤2/5 criteria met): **0 points**

**Examples:**
- San Marco Village (Class B, 180 units, Jacksonville): 20 pts (proven success)
- Lory of Braden River (Class B-, 250 units, Bradenton): 20 pts (proven success)
- Generic Class A: 0 pts (wrong model)

### 4. Operator Track Record (20 points max)

**Requirement:** Historical portfolio posted target returns (12%+)

**Scoring Logic:**
- Lexerd portfolio avg (18% IRR): **20 points**
- 15–18% IRR: **15 points**
- 12–15% IRR: **10 points**
- <12% IRR or no track record: **0 points**

**Data sources:**
- Prior deals (San Marco, Lory, etc.)
- Lexerd LP reports
- Third-party market surveys (CBRE, Marcus & Millichap)

**Why:** Historical performance predicts future execution. Properties with experienced operators close faster, execute better.

## Using This Rubric in Decision-Making

### Screening Phase (Deal Flow to Shortlist)

1. Score all prospects using this rubric
2. Grade A & B deals → Fast-track to diligence
3. Grade C deals → Flag for closer review (may pass)
4. Grade D deals → Pass unless thesis pivot

### Diligence Phase (Shortlist to Underwriting)

1. Rubric score informs due diligence depth:
   - Grade A: Light diligence, focus on execution
   - Grade B: Standard diligence
   - Grade C: Deep diligence, thesis-risk mitigation
2. Actual market/property data may improve or worsen score
3. Final investment decision driven by underwriting, not rubric score

### Post-Close Review

1. Compare actual performance to rubric score
2. Refine rubric if predictions diverge from reality
3. Update benchmarks as market conditions change

## Rubric Calibration Notes

**This rubric is tunable:**
- Weight adjustments: If Model upside becomes more important, increase Model weight from 40% to 50%
- Threshold adjustments: If secondary market employment growth declines, adjust minimum from 2.0% to 1.8%
- Anchor updates: If new employment anchor emerges, update anchor types
- Benchmark refreshes: Update expense ratio benchmarks quarterly (real-time CoStar data)

**Annual review recommended:** Market conditions evolve; rubric should reflect current reality.
