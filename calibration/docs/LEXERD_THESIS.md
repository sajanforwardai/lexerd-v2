## Executive Summary

**Lexerd Capital Management** acquires small-to-mid-market multifamily properties in Southeast and Sun Belt secondary markets, where employment anchors (military, medical, university) drive long-term demand fundamentals. We identify value-add opportunities—properties with below-market occupancy (80–95%) and above-benchmark operating costs (>28% expense ratio)—acquired off-market at below-market pricing. Our proprietary 3M Model (Market growth signals + operational upside + management execution) targets 12%+ levered IRR over 5-year holds, delivering 8%+ annual distributions to LPs plus capital appreciation.

**Investment Sweet Spot:** $10M–$100M total capital per deal, with $2M–$20M equity checks, acquiring properties <$50M that compete in a mid-market segment avoiding mega-fund competition.

## Part 1: The Market Thesis (30% Weight)

### Strategic Context

The multifamily market is bifurcated:
- **Tier-1 metros** (NYC, LA, SF, Chicago, Boston, DC): Saturated with institutional capital; cap rates compressed to 4.0–5.0%; limited value-add upside
- **Tier-2 metros** (Austin, Nashville, Raleigh, Charlotte, Phoenix): Growing, but increasingly competitive; cap rates 5.0–6.5%
- **Secondary markets** (<1M population MSAs): Underserved by institutional capital; fundamentals driven by secular employment anchors; cap rates 6.5–9.0%; clear value-add runway

**Lexerd's thesis:** Tier-3+ secondary markets in the Southeast and Sun Belt (GA, FL, AL, SC, NC, TX, KS) offer the best risk-adjusted returns: strong fundamental demand + moderate competition + realistic value-add execution.

### Market Characteristics We Target

#### 1. Employment Anchor Strength

We target markets with **secular employment anchors** — industries/employers that won't disappear in a downturn and generate consistent job growth:

- **Military bases** (e.g., Fort Eisenhower in Augusta, GA — $2.4B economic impact, 50+ year horizon)
- **Medical centers** (regional hospitals, medical clusters; aging demographic = permanent tailwind)
- **Universities** (stable employment, student housing demand, research jobs)
- **Manufacturing hubs** (e.g., automotive, aerospace; regional specialization = sticky)

**Why anchors matter:** These employers provide recession-resistant demand for workforce housing. When oil prices collapse, tech crashes, or retail dies, military bases, hospitals, and universities keep hiring and moving employees into rental housing.

**Signal:** Market has at least 1–2 anchors in top 5 employers.

#### 2. Employment Growth Rate

**Minimum:** 2% YoY employment growth (national avg is 1–1.5%)

**Why:** Employment growth drives net migration, occupancy stability, and rent growth. Properties in 3%+ employment growth markets outperform during value-add execution and exit.

**Example:** Fargo, ND (military base + tech) grew at 2.8% YoY 2015–2023, supporting consistent rent growth even during COVID. Austin, TX (tech hub) grew at 3.5%+, driving 8%+ annual rent growth.

#### 3. Population Growth Rate

**Minimum:** 1.5% YoY population growth

**Why:** In-migration supports long-term occupancy. Markets losing population (Rust Belt cities) have structural headwinds. Markets gaining population have tailwinds.

**Lexerd's experience:** Bradenton, FL (Lory of Braden River) grew at 2.1% YoY; exit at 96% occupancy with 37.7% appreciation. Jacksonville, FL (San Marco Village) grew at 1.8% YoY; exit with 30.9% NOI improvement.

#### 4. Cap-Rate Spread vs. National Average

**Minimum:** >200 basis points above national average

**Why:** Spread indicates valuation opportunity. If national average cap rate is 6.0%, we target markets trading at 8.0%+.

**Mechanics:** Higher cap rate = lower purchase price (for same NOI) = more upside from NOI improvement. Example:
- Property with $500K annual NOI
  - National avg (6% cap): purchase price = $500K / 0.06 = $8.33M
  - Secondary market (8% cap): purchase price = $500K / 0.08 = $6.25M
  - **Spread savings: $2.08M equity on same asset**

#### 5. Refinance Signals (Loan Maturity Wall)

**Target:** Properties with loans maturing in 1–3 years (especially in rising-rate environment)

**Why:** Maturing loans create motivation for sellers/lenders. Owners facing refinance at 6%+ rates (vs. 3% origination rate) become motivated to sell off-market rather than refinance. Lenders managing distressed pools need capital solutions.

**2026 market:** $162B multifamily loan maturity wall (Freddie Mac data); South Atlantic has $9.4B in maturing securitized debt. This creates a deal sourcing tailwind: motivated sellers abound.

## Part 2: The Model Thesis (40% Weight)

### Value-Add Playbook

Lexerd's core value creation comes from **operational improvement**, not market appreciation. We buy properties where the incumbent operator is underperforming and we can systematically improve NOI.

#### Property Entry Profile

**Unit Count:** 70–300 units
- 70 units = minimum threshold for institutional PM efficiency
- 300 units = inflection point where complexity compounds (larger properties need more back-office, more staff specialization)
- **Sweet spot: 150–250 units** = proven Lory playbook, repeatable execution

**Property Class:** B / B- (10–30 years old)
- **Class A (luxury, 0–5 years):** Fully optimized by prior owner; little operational upside; high prices, low cap rates (4.5–5.5%); attracts mega-funds
- **Class B (institutional, 10–25 years old):** Solid bones, good locations; realistic PM-replacement upside; cap rates 5.5–7.5%
  - **Lexerd's target:** Class B in 10–20 year range; newer enough to not have major capex pending, old enough to have operational inefficiency
- **Class B- (fair, 15–30 years old):** Fair condition; higher operational upside but execution risk increases; cap rates 6.5–8.5%
  - **Acceptable if:** Clear value-add thesis exists (e.g., known-broken PM, deferred maintenance quantified, rent <market)
- **Class C (distressed, 35+ years):** High capex, execution risk; typically avoid unless distressed seller = deep discount

#### Entry Signals: Below-Market Occupancy + High Expense Ratio

**Occupancy Sweet Spot:** 80–95% (below market average of 90–95%)

**Why:** Occupancy gap is the clearest signal of incumbent operator failure. Well-run properties in growing markets sustain 94%+ occupancy. Properties at 85% occupancy have:
1. Operational issue (broken marketing, poor customer service, deferred maintenance visible to residents)
2. Capital opportunity (fix it, occupancy rebounds to 92%+)

**Expense Ratio Sweet Spot:** >28% of Gross Rental Income (vs. market benchmark of 23–28%)

Why expense ratios matter:
- **Management fees:** Well-run PMs charge 5–7% of gross revenue; broken PMs tolerate higher fees or bloated staffing
- **Utilities:** Deferred energy efficiency (old HVAC, bad insulation, poor water management) pushes utility costs to 8%+
- **Maintenance:** Deferred upkeep = higher breakdown costs; well-run properties invest in preventive maintenance
- **Labor:** Legacy staffing; paying longtime employees above-market wages; redundant positions

**Typical Lexerd entry:** 30–33% expense ratio, benchmark is 27%, upside is 200–300 bps.

**Math:**
- 200-unit property, $1.2M annual rent revenue
- Current expense ratio 30% = $360K annual expenses
- Benchmark expense ratio 27% = $324K annual expenses
- **Annual NOI improvement: $36K** (just from bringing expenses to benchmark)
- At 7.5% cap rate, that's **$480K additional property value**
- **Levered ROI:** if acquired with 40% LTV, that's $480K / ($X equity) return

#### Entry Signals: Below-Market Rent (Upside Potential)

Properties renting below market have upside:
- **Market average rent:** $1,300/unit/month
- **Entry rent:** $1,200/unit/month (8% below market)
- **Upside:** Bring rents to market over 18–24 months through: (a) tenant turnover, (b) lease-up at market rates, (c) in-place raises

**Mechanics:**
- 200-unit property at $1,200/unit = $240K annual rent revenue
- Market rent $1,300/unit = $260K annual rent revenue
- **Rent upside: $20K annually** (8% of base rent)
- Over 5-year hold, compounds to 40%+ value creation if cap rate holds

#### Property Management Type

**Preferred:** Third-party managed

**Why:** Third-party PM can be replaced. Owner-managed properties often have cultural barriers to change (owner resists new systems, staff has tenure relationships, etc.). Third-party PMs are contractors and can be swapped post-close.

**Integration:** Lexerd partners with **First Communities** (existing operational depth) and deploys the **Lory rebranding playbook** — a documented, repeatable methodology for:
1. PM transition (75% staff stays, 25% turned over for performance)
2. Utility cost optimization (energy audits, vendor consolidation)
3. Lease-up strategy (pricing, marketing, resident experience)
4. Capital plan prioritization (ROI-first approach)

## Part 3: The Management Thesis (30% Weight)

### Execution Risk is Real

Properties don't create value by themselves. Value is created by **management**, post-acquisition. Lexerd's thesis rests on three pillars:

#### 1. Operator Selection & PM Replacement

**Best predictor of success:** Pre-close, we assess the incumbent operator:
- Is the PM experienced in value-add (or cost-reduction-focused)?
- What is staff tenure? (High tenure = cultural resistance to change)
- What is the PM fee structure? (Can we re-negotiate?)

**Lexerd approach:**
- Keep experienced maintenance/operations staff (save them)
- Replace underperforming leasing/management layer (20–30% turnover)
- Deploy new systems (rent-setting, utility management, customer experience) via First Communities

**Success rate:** In our historical deals, PM replacement + systematic execution = 200–300 bps of annual NOI improvement within 18–24 months.

#### 2. First Communities Integration

Lexerd partners with **First Communities**, one of the largest apartment managers in the US. Post-close, First Communities brings:
- Utility procurement (scale advantage saves 5–10% on utilities)
- Staffing benchmark (know what a "good" maintenance cost looks like)
- Lease-up playbook (proven rent-setting and marketing strategy)
- Systems (accounting, resident communication, maintenance scheduling)

**Management score:** Properties that integrate well with First Communities' model score higher than those with complications (union contracts, complex tenant mix, niche property types).

#### 3. Lory Rebranding Playbook

Lexerd's owned rebranding platform **Lory** encodes successful value-add playbooks from prior deals:
- **San Marco Village** (Jacksonville, 2016–2018): PM replacement → 30.9% NOI improvement
- **Lory of Braden River** (Bradenton, 2017–2021): PM + rent growth → 37.7% appreciation

Lory works for: Class B/B-, 100–300 units, mid-market, PM replacement opportunity, rent-growth markets.

**Not applicable for:** Luxury (already optimized), tiny (<50 units, too small for scale), highly specialized (student housing, senior, military-specific).

## Part 4: Financial Constraints (Hard Boxes)

### Deal Size

**Equity per deal:** $2M–$20M
- **Why $2M minimum:** Smaller checks don't justify due diligence cost and management overhead
- **Why $20M maximum:** Keeps deals mid-market, avoids size/complexity of mega-deals

**Total capital per deal:** $10M–$100M
- **Why $10M minimum:** Most multifamily lenders want $10M+ DSCR pools
- **Why $100M maximum:** Keeps LTV manageable (~60–70%), avoids over-leverage

**Acquisition price:** <$50M
- **Why:** Sweet spot where mega-funds (Blackstone, KKR) don't compete; regional PE and family offices compete
- **Cap rates:** Typically 6.5–8.5% for Class B in secondary markets

### Financing Assumptions

**Loan-to-Value:** 60–70% (leaving 30–40% equity buffer)
- **Why conservative LTV:** Allows DSCR 1.1–1.3x (some stress), refinance flexibility
- **Why not aggressive:** Protects against rate shocks, downside risk

**Expected DSCR post-stabilization:** 1.25–1.35x
- **Current DSCR (entry):** Often 1.1–1.2x (property is stressed, that's why we buy it)
- **Post-NOI improvement:** DSCR improves as expenses fall and occupancy rises

### Return Target

**Levered IRR:** 12%+ over 5-year hold

**Components:**
- Debt paydown: 2–3% annual IRR contribution
- Cap rate expansion (if multiples expand in exit market): 1–2% annual
- Rent growth: 2–3% annual (in-place rent growth + new leases)
- NOI improvement (operational): 3–4% annual (this is Lexerd's value-add edge)

**Example math (200-unit property):**
- Entry: $9M purchase price, $550K NOI, 6.1% cap rate
- Year 1–2: PM replacement, NOI → $700K (+27%), expense ratio 28% → 25%
- Year 5 exit: $9.3M NOI, 7.2% cap rate (market has normalized), exit price = $9.3M / 0.072 = $12.9M
- **Gross multiple: 1.43x** (not including debt paydown)
- **Levered IRR: ~15%** (depends on debt terms)
